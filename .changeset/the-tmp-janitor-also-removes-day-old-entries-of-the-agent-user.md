`a11ign-tmp-prune.timer` now also removes the entries of `/tmp` that the agent user owns and that nothing has written for 24 hours, whatever they are called (A11-348). Until now the janitor removed only named families (test fixtures; review leftovers and scratchpads wait for an announced cycle), so the workers' own logs, body files and `a11-<n>-*` scratch directories were "unrecognised" and stayed, and the board asked a person once `/tmp` held over 1,000 entries. The new `aged` family is every direct child of `/tmp` that no other family owns, except a `claude-<uid>` scratchpad root; it is walked only by a run that names it (`--timer`); an entry is removed only when the agent's uid owns it, the newest write in its top two levels is over 24 hours old, and no running process holds it open, stands in it, names it in its argv or environment, or runs it as its executable, or has a unix socket bound inside it (the last read from `/proc/net/unix`, since a listening socket is not a path in any fd list); an entry on another filesystem than `/tmp` (a mount point) is left alone. It runs under the same budget as before (300 removals a minute, a pause after each, a directory renamed to `.prune-tmp-doomed-*` first), plus a 30 s wall-clock bound per run, and entries whose own mtime is inside the window cost one `lstat` and none of the read cap, so a crowd of young entries cannot keep an old one from being reached. The unit's `ExecStart` is now `prune-tmp.ts --apply --timer` (the fixtures, the unfinished trees and the aged entries); `--fixtures-only` still means the fixtures alone, and a run with neither flag still lists and removes the review, scratchpad and fixture families only. The board's "/tmp holds N entries" alert names `a11ign-tmp-prune.timer`, says what it removes and where its count is logged (`journalctl --user -u a11ign-tmp-prune.service`); it still clears when the count is back under 1,000. A symlink directly under `/tmp` is aged by its own time, not its target's.
